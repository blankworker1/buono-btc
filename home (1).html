Seminare un'economia circolare

3 ottobre 2026

Un'economia circolare è la destinazione. BUONO riguarda il passo che viene prima: seminare bitcoin in una città, e continuare a farlo.

Continua a leggereChiudi

Oggi il flusso è semplice. Entrano euro: un organizzatore paga in anticipo a un locale un certo numero di suoi prodotti. Circolano bitcoin: i clienti pagano quei prodotti in sats, e i sats tornano all'organizzatore. Lungo la strada una parte dei sats resta nei wallet di chi ha appena ricevuto i suoi primi. È proprio questo lo scopo, ed è anche il motivo per cui il giro non è del tutto efficiente.

Tre attività procedono in parallelo per farlo girare.

Prodotti prepagati. Il locale viene pagato in anticipo in euro e non ha nulla da perdere. Incassa in sats con un telefono e non deve mai tenere bitcoin.

Card regalo. Una card mette qualche sats nelle mani di chi non ne ha mai avuti. La persona installa un wallet, riscatta il regalo e lo spende in un locale.

Contanti in cambio di sats, da persona a persona. Agli incontri periodici di BUONO, e agli incontri bitcoin che già si tengono in città, si cambiano contanti in sats direttamente tra persone, e col tempo forse a una piccola macchina Lightning. Gli euro raccolti così servono al lotto successivo di prodotti.

È la terza attività a rendere sostenibile la semina. Senza, ogni nuovo lotto ha bisogno di denaro fresco dall'esterno. Con essa, gli euro che comprano sats diventano gli euro che prepagano i locali, e i sats che tornano dalla cassa ripartono verso la persona successiva.

Non è ancora un'economia circolare. Per quella serve la piena partecipazione dei locali: che tengano i sats incassati e li spendano a loro volta. È un passo che per ora va oltre ciò che BUONO si propone. Quello che può fare è questo: quando un locale sarà pronto, ci saranno già persone vicine con dei sats in tasca e l'abitudine di spenderli.

Zero debiti

L'offerta sta in una riga. Vendi un lotto di qualcosa che già prepari, dieci pizze, venti cocktail, due notti, e vieni pagato per tutto oggi, in euro.

Cosa comporta. Si concordano un prodotto e un prezzo. Ricevi il pagamento, con i documenti che useresti per qualsiasi ordine anticipato. Ti vengono dati un codice QR per lo staff e un adesivo per la vetrina. La preparazione è tutta qui.

Una vendita. Un cliente chiede di pagare in bitcoin. Il cameriere apre la cassa sul telefono, sceglie la quantità e mostra un codice al cliente. Il cliente paga, lo schermo diventa verde, e quel prodotto viene segnato sul conto come già pagato. Il resto del tavolo si paga con carta o contanti come sempre.

Cosa non devi fare mai. Non tieni bitcoin, non impari a usare un wallet, non guardi il prezzo, non paghi commissioni e non aspetti di essere pagato.

Zero debiti. Bitcoin è denaro che non è il debito di nessuno. Non è la promessa di una banca: o ce l'hai o non ce l'hai. Un locale BUONO si trova nella stessa posizione. Niente è a credito, niente può essere stornato, e nessuno torna più tardi a chiedere soldi.

Una cosa la devi: le pizze. Sei stato pagato per dieci, quindi ne servi dieci. È un debito di impasto, non di euro, e sono pizze che avresti fatto comunque.

Se un giorno vorrai tenerti i sats, quella porta è aperta. Niente di tutto questo dipende da quella scelta.

Seeding a Circular Economy

3 October 2026

A circular economy is the destination. BUONO is about the step before it: planting bitcoin in a town, and keeping that planting going.

Read moreClose

Today the flow is simple. Euros go in: an organiser pays a venue in advance for a batch of its products. Bitcoin circulates: customers pay for those products in sats, and the sats return to the organiser. Along the way some sats stay in the wallets of people who have just received their first. That last part is the point, and it is also why the loop is not fully efficient.

Three activities run side by side to keep it turning.

Prepaid products. A venue is paid up front in euros and has nothing to lose. It takes payment in sats with a phone and never has to hold bitcoin.

Gift cards. A card puts a few sats into the hands of someone who has never had any. They install a wallet, claim the gift, and spend it at a venue.

Cash for sats, person to person. At regular BUONO meet-ups, and at existing bitcoin meet-ups in the city, people swap cash for sats directly with one another, and in time perhaps at a small Lightning machine. Euros raised this way go towards the next batch of products.

The third activity is what makes the seeding sustainable. Without it, every new batch needs fresh money from outside. With it, the euros that buy sats become the euros that prepay venues, and the sats that come back through the till go out again to the next person.

This is not yet a circular economy. That needs venues to take part fully: to keep the sats they earn and spend them in turn. That step is beyond what BUONO sets out to do for now. What it can do is make sure that when a venue is ready, there are already people nearby with sats in their pockets and the habit of spending them.

Zero debiti

3 October 2026

A venue that joins BUONO is paid before its first customer arrives, and never owes anyone money.

Read moreClose

The offer fits in one line. You sell a batch of something you already make, ten pizzas, twenty cocktails, two nights' stay, and you are paid for all of it today, in euros.

What is involved. You agree a product and a price. You receive the payment, with the paperwork you would use for any advance order. You get a QR code for your staff and a sticker for the window. That is the whole setup.

A sale. A customer asks to pay in bitcoin. The waiter opens the till on a phone, chooses how many, and shows the customer a code. The customer pays, the screen turns green, and that item is marked on the bill as already paid. Everything else on the table is paid by card or cash as usual.

What you never do. You never hold bitcoin, learn a wallet, watch a price, pay a commission, or wait to be paid.

Zero debiti. Bitcoin is money that is nobody's debt. It is not a promise from a bank: you hold it or you don't. A BUONO venue stands in the same place. Nothing is on credit, nothing can be reversed, and nobody comes back later asking for money.

There is one thing you do owe: the pizzas. You have been paid for ten, so you serve ten. It is a debt in dough, not in euros, and one you were always going to make.

If one day you want to keep the sats yourself, that door is open. Nothing here depends on it.

I noticed the sticker first. A green circle in a pizzeria window, an orange B, the word BUONO, and underneath, in English, "accepted here". I had no idea what it meant, which is a good reason to find out.

That evening there was a meet-up in a bar off the main square. A dozen people, two laptops, more talk about the town than about technology. Someone handed me a square card with the same logo and a number on it: Nº 0042. "Peel the sticker when you're ready," she said.

I was ready about four minutes later. Under the sticker was a code. My phone's camera opened a page, the page offered me a wallet, and the wallet told me I had been given a gift. I also swapped a twenty-euro note with the man next to me for a little more. That was the whole of my financial education, and it took less time than ordering a drink.

The next day I went back to the pizzeria to see what would happen. There was nothing about bitcoin on the menu. When the waiter came I asked, as casually as I could, what the Bitcoin Special was today.

I was hoping for a raised eyebrow. I got none. "Sausage and wild fennel," he said, and wrote one line on his pad. It came, it was good, and at the end he fetched a phone from behind the counter, tapped twice and turned the screen towards me. I scanned it. His screen went green. My friend paid for the wine by card, the way we would have split any bill.

I asked the same question at a gelateria the next afternoon. Same sticker, same absence of drama: the special was a cup of whatever the gelataio had made that morning, fig and a ricotta I would go back for. I asked the owner what she made of it all. "I was paid for those cups last month," she said. "You're just collecting one."

Nobody mentioned the price of bitcoin all weekend. The card is still in my wallet.

This is an illustration of a visit, written to show how BUONO works from the customer's side.

The Bitcoin Special

3 October 2026

Most people want bitcoin to be ordinary. We'd like it to be special.

The usual hope is that paying in bitcoin becomes as unremarkable as tapping a card. That may come. In the meantime, an unremarkable way to pay gives nobody a reason to try it.

So BUONO starts from the other end. In a BUONO venue, bitcoin buys something you cannot get any other way: the Bitcoin Special. It is not on the menu. You have to ask for it by name, and the answer changes from day to day.

At the pizzeria it is whatever the pizzaiolo feels like making. At the bar it is the cocktail of the evening. At the gelateria it is the gelataio's pick of the day's flavours.

The price is fixed and you are told it before you order, and the waiter can say what is in it. What you don't get is a choice, and that is the pleasure of it.

For the venue it is freedom. The Special is paid for in advance as a pizza, a drink or a cup, not as a recipe.

One question works everywhere. Learn it once and the town opens up a little: gelato in the afternoon, a cocktail at aperitivo, pizza at night.

"What is the Bitcoin Special today?" It is a small thing to ask. It is also how bitcoin stops being a way to pay and becomes a reason to walk in.
